  \begin{abstract}
    Continuing from the companion paper Q6a, we assemble the effective spacetime geometry that the admissibility
    framework of Foundation~M yields conditionally, on a supplied Heisenberg carrier.
    Q5a shows that the published admissibility forms supply no spatial limit operator (the zero form for uniformly
    bounded weights; no common scalar normalisation under [H-depth] and [H-$w'$]), so the operator
    $-A\partial_x^2$ enters as the hypothesis~[H-L] of Q5b.
    Under~[H-L] and the open lifting hypothesis~[H-lift], the effective operator on
    $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ has a rank-three co-metric
    $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, 0)$ (Q5b Theorem~5.2), whose non-degenerate block has signature $(-,+,+)$, with
    stipulated signs, under the hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] (Q5b Theorem~6.1); the signature
    $(-,+,+,+)$ needs a supplied rank-four extension (open problem Q5b-O2).
    The Born–Infeld paper obtains a Newtonian exterior profile on a spherically symmetric, asymptotically flat
    continuum, and the Schwarzschild form only with external dynamical input; on the Heisenberg carrier both need a
    supplied extension with these properties, which Q5b does not provide, and the horizon reading is interpretive.
    On Riemannian backgrounds, the Einstein coefficient of the Gravity paper is a matching datum.
    Each arrow of the chain $\Pi_q \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{H\text{-}L}]} \mathcal{L}_\Pi
    \xrightarrow{[\mathrm{H\text{-}lift}]} g^{\mu\nu}_{(3)} \xrightarrow{\text{Q5b-O2}} g^{\mu\nu} \to G_{\mu\nu}$
    carries its own condition: the posited limit; the open [H-lift], with a stipulated temporal term; a supplied
    extension, Lorentzian if diagonal with $A_z > 0$ under [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$]; a Lorentzian form of the
    Riemannian Gravity argument.
    \textbf{Interpretive reading.}
    The construction reads spacetime geometry as an effective propagation structure rather than as primitive
    substrate data; it does not show that admissibility selects it.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
  \label{sec:intro}

  \subsection*{Position in the programme}

    The Cosmochrony programme~\cite{Cosmochrony} derives physical structure from a static relational substrate
    $\chi$ through a non-injective projection $\Pi$ onto an observable space.
    The companion paper Q6a~\cite{Beau2026q6a} derives gauge charges, the gauge group, and Yang\textendash Mills
    dynamics from the fibre structure of~$\Pi$.
    The present paper addresses the complementary macroscopic question: what effective geometry is selected when the
    bounded admissibility constraints of Foundation~M~\cite{Beau2026m} are imposed on a spatially varying
    projection?

    The geometric chain is conditional at every step.
    Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} shows that, with the pipeline's initial vector, the canonical filtration of the admissible
    fibre on the supplied Heisenberg carrier is a growing toric Fourier window; that, for uniformly bounded weights,
    the published admissibility forms converge on it to the zero form; and that, under its Hypotheses~[H-depth]
    and~[H-$w'$], no common scalar normalisation produces a non-trivial toric differential operator.
    The spatial limit operator $\mathcal{L}_\Pi = -A\,\partial_x^2$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ is therefore not established;
    Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} posits it as the hypothesis~[H-L].
    Independently of~[H-L], Q5b shows that on balls of radius $n \ll \sqrt{q}$ the rescaled BFS shells of $G_q$
    converge, in the Gromov\textendash Hausdorff sense, to the sphere foliation of a sub-Finsler
    Carnot\textendash Carath\'eodory metric on $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$, of homogeneous dimension
    $D_{\mathrm{hom}} = 4$.
    Under~[H-L] and the open lifting hypothesis~[H-lift], the principal symbol of the effective operator on
    $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ gives a co-metric of rank three; its extension to a
    four-dimensional Lorentzian co-metric is the open problem Q5b-O2.

    The task of the present paper is to examine how this conditional geometry connects to the Einstein equations of
    general relativity, and under which further inputs.
    Three conditional statements are discussed, each requiring a supplied rank-four extension of the co-metric:
    (i) the stationary exterior of a localised obstruction, for which the Born\textendash Infeld paper obtains, on a
    spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat continuum, the Newtonian profile from flux conservation and the
    Schwarzschild form only with external dynamical input~\cite{Beau2026c}; on the Heisenberg carrier, both need a
    supplied extension with these properties (§\ref{sec:schwarzschild}); (ii) an interpretive reading of the
    horizon (§\ref{sec:horizon}); (iii) the relation to the Einstein equations through the spectral entropy functional
    of the Gravity paper~\cite{Beau2026h}, whose Einstein coefficient is a matching datum (§\ref{sec:einstein}).

  \subsection*{What is and is not derived in this paper}

    This paper assembles the conditional \emph{structure} of effective spacetime geometry from the admissibility
    framework.
    It does not derive the precise numerical value of Newton's constant $G_N$, nor does it establish the hypotheses
    inherited from Q5b.
    Its geometric statements are conditional on~[H-L] (Q5b Hypothesis~1.1, not established), on~[H-lift]
    (Q5b Hypothesis~4.1, open; Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} gives sufficient conditions and does not discharge it), on the
    stipulated temporal term of the effective operator and, for the signature, on~[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] (Q5b
    Theorem~6.1).
    Every statement that needs a non-degenerate four-dimensional metric is further conditional on a supplied rank-four
    extension of the co-metric (open problem Q5b-O2).
    The Newtonian profile and the Schwarzschild form further need a supplied rank-four extension that is spherically
    symmetric and asymptotically flat.
    Q5b provides none, and no left-invariant extension, in particular no diagonal one
    $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$, qualifies: a left-invariant metric on $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ is not
    flat (Q5b Remark~6.2) and, being homogeneous, is not asymptotically flat.
    Whether an extension that is not left-invariant has these properties, and how it would relate to the Carnot
    structure of homogeneous dimension~$4$, is open.
    For a static line element $-f_t\,dt^2 + f_r\,dr^2 + r^2\,d\Omega^2$, the Schwarzschild form also needs exterior
    homogeneity $A_r \equiv 1$ as a hypothesis and, as external dynamical input, the closure $f_t f_r = 1$ and the
    exact temporal identification $f_t = 1 + 2\Phi$ with $\Phi_0 = 0$~\cite{Beau2026c}.
    Open directions are collected in Section~\ref{sec:open}.

  \subsection{What Q5a establishes, and the spatial limit hypothesis [H-L]}
    \label{sec:q5a}

    Foundation~M~\cite{Beau2026m} takes two supplied inputs.
    The first is the relational group $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$.
    The second is a Heisenberg carrier for it, the Heisenberg\textendash Schr\"odinger representation $\pi_c$ on
    $\mathcal{H}_c = L^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ at a fixed non-trivial central character.
    Identifying the admissible fibre with $\mathcal{H}_c$ is a modelling dictionary rather than a theorem.
    The carrier is not selected by the admissibility axioms: together with Born\textendash Infeld parity they admit an
    explicit $\mathfrak{S}_3$ countermodel~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026HeisenbergStructure}.
    It is therefore a supplied input here, and its associated Weil action of the symplectic automorphism group is a
    further representation, which is not used below.
    The discrete admissibility filter $\Pi_q$ acts on the orbit span $\Omega_n^{(c)}$ through the Born\textendash Infeld
    admissibility constraint $A_n \leq c_{\mathrm{BI}}/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$ (Level~4 of~\cite{Beau2026m}).
    Foundation~M states the saturation threshold of its Axioms~A3\textendash A4 as the capacity axiom [A-cap]
    of~\cite{Beau2026c}, with the Born\textendash Infeld form as an explicit additional input.

    Q5a~\cite{Beau2026q5a} determines what the canonical filtration converges to:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item[(Q5a-1)] with the pipeline's initial vector, the filtration stage $\Omega_n$ is exactly the toric Fourier
      window $\mathrm{span}\{e^{2\pi i b x/q} : |b| \le \min(n, (q-1)/2)\}$ (Q5a Theorem~3.1);
      \item[(Q5a-2)] if the admissibility weights are uniformly bounded, the published admissibility forms tend to zero
      on the filtration stages at rate $q^{-2}$, uniformly on norm-bounded sets, and their Mosco limit, along any
      isometric identification of the stages with subspaces of a fixed Hilbert space whose ranges have asymptotically
      dense union, is the zero form (Q5a Theorem~5.1);
      \item[(Q5a-3)] under Hypotheses~[H-depth] (diverging saturation depth) and~[H-$w'$] (positive finite limits of the
      modulation and translation weights), no common scalar normalisation of the forms produces, on toric targets, a
      limit containing both a finite derivative term and the modulation sector (Q5a Theorem~6.1).
    \end{itemize}
    The spatial limit operator consumed by Q5b is therefore not established.
    Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} formalises it as an explicit hypothesis.
    \begin{itemize}
      \item[{[H-L]}] (Q5b Hypothesis~1.1; not established.)
      There exists a normalisation of the admissibility structure under which the admissibility forms converge to the
      second-order operator
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:Lpi-shadow}
        \mathcal{L}_\Pi \;=\; -A\,\partial_x^2
        \quad\text{on}\quad L^2(\mathbb{R}),
        \qquad A > 0.
      \end{equation}
    \end{itemize}
    Q5b records that no source of~[H-L] is identified in the corpus, and that Q5a shows it to fail for the published
    normalisation (for uniformly bounded weights) and for every common scalar rescaling of it on toric targets (under
    [H-depth] and [H-$w'$]).
    Every statement of the present paper that consumes~\eqref{eq:Lpi-shadow} is conditional on~[H-L].
    The operator~\eqref{eq:Lpi-shadow} is one-dimensional; its relation to the homogeneous four-dimensional structure
    carried by $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ is the content of Q5b, under the further hypothesis~[H-lift].

  \subsection{From the 1D shadow to a four-dimensional Carnot geometry}
    \label{sec:q5b}

    Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} establishes a geometric convergence result that does not depend on~[H-L], and two results,
    conditional on~[H-L], [H-lift] and, for the signature, [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$], that relate~$\mathcal{L}_\Pi$ to
    a rank-three effective co-metric.

    \paragraph{Carnot convergence.}
      For the integer Heisenberg group, the BFS balls satisfy $c_1 n^4 \le |B_n| \le c_2 n^4$
      (Bass\textendash Guivarc'h) and $|B_n| \sim C n^4$ (Pansu), the homogeneous dimension of the Carnot group
      $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ being $D_{\mathrm{hom}} = 4$ (Q5b Theorem~2.1).
      On balls of radius $n = \lfloor q^\alpha \rfloor$ with $0 < \alpha < 1/2$, where the BFS balls of
      $G_q = \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ coincide with those of the integer Heisenberg group, the rescaled
      BFS metric converges in the Gromov\textendash Hausdorff sense to a sub-Finsler Carnot\textendash Carath\'eodory
      ball of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$, as a structural consequence of Pansu's asymptotic cone theorem (Q5b
      Theorem~3.2~\cite{Beau2026q5b}).
      This convergence does not depend on~[H-L].
      The limiting geometry carries Hausdorff and spectral dimension~$4$.

    \paragraph{Lifting hypothesis.}
      Q5b introduces Hypothesis~[H-lift] (Hypothesis~4.1 of~\cite{Beau2026q5b}): the operator $\mathcal{L}_\Pi$
      of~\eqref{eq:Lpi-shadow} posited by~[H-L] is $A$ times the image, under the Schr\"odinger representation~$\pi_1$
      at unit central character, of the kinetic sector of the nonnegative operator $-\Delta_H$, where
      $\Delta_H = \widetilde{X}^2 + \widetilde{Y}^2$ is the sub-Laplacian of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$, restricted
      to the admissible (low-energy) subspace of $L^2(\mathbb{R})$.
      Its object exists only under~[H-L].
      The Plancherel decomposition of $L^2(\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R}))$ into Schr\"odinger representations
      $\pi_\lambda$ acting on $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ is classical in the harmonic analysis of nilpotent Lie groups
      (Corwin\textendash Greenleaf, Folland, Stein), and Q5b names it as a possible route (open problem Q5b-O1).
      The main open step is a direct proof that the limit posited by~[H-L] coincides, up to the factor~$A$, with the
      kinetic sector of $-d\pi_1(\Delta_H)$.
      Hypothesis~[H-lift] is open.
      Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} proves that, under its premises [K] and [R], the forms Mosco-converge to the free kinetic
      form, and states that this does not discharge~[H-lift].

    \paragraph{From the principal symbol to a rank-three co-metric.}
      \label{par:cometric-metric}
      Under~[H-L] and~[H-lift], the effective operator $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ on
      $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ has a principal symbol
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:principal-symbol}
        \sigma_2(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}})(x, k) \;=\; A^{\mu\nu}(x)\,k_\mu k_\nu,
      \end{equation}
      where $A^{\mu\nu}$ is a symmetric contravariant tensor on the cotangent bundle $T^*M$ with entries $-A_\tau$ in
      the temporal sector and $A_H$ in the horizontal sector.
      The principal symbol is \emph{degenerate}: at the sub-Riemannian level inherited from Q5b, $A^{\mu\nu}$ has a
      null direction along the central generator~$Z$ of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$, reflecting the horizontal-only
      propagation of the leading-order operator.
      Consequently, $A^{\mu\nu}$ does not admit a direct inverse on $TM$ and does not itself define a metric tensor
      at the principal-symbol level.

      In the left-invariant frame $(\partial_\tau, \widetilde{X}, \widetilde{Y}, \widetilde{Z})$, Q5b Theorem~5.2
      gives, under~[H-L] and~[H-lift], the leading-order co-metric
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:metric-from-symbol}
        g^{\mu\nu}(p) \;=\; \mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau,\; A_H,\; A_H,\; 0),
      \end{equation}
      read off from the principal symbol, the temporal part of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ being stipulated.
      This tensor has rank three.
      No term of lower differential order in $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ changes it: the sub-Laplacian has no
      first-order part and a rank-two principal symbol (Q5b Remark~5.4; Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}, Proposition~3.1).
      A non-degenerate co-metric therefore cannot be obtained by completing~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol} through
      sub-principal corrections.
      It requires a new operator containing a central term of homogeneous degree four, together with the length
      scale that such a term carries (Q5b Remark~5.5); this is the open problem Q5b-O2.
      No value of $A_\tau$, $A_H$ or of a central coefficient $A_z$ is established (open problems Q5b-O2 for $A_z$
      and Q5b-O3).
      The co-metric is a derived object encoding how admissible modes would propagate in the continuum limit posited
      by~[H-L], not an independent dynamical variable.

    \paragraph{Lorentzian signature (conditional, on the non-degenerate block).}
      \label{par:signature-origin}
      The signature of the effective co-metric is not established within the present paper.
      Q5b Theorem~6.1 applies the hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp] of~\cite{Beau2026c} to the reduction of
      $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ to functions independent of the central variable,
      $L_{\mathrm{red}} = -A_\tau\partial_\tau^2 + A_H(\partial_x^2 + \partial_y^2)$; this application is denoted
      [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$].
      Its three clauses posit that $L_{\mathrm{red}}$ is second order in~$\tau$, that its principal symbol is
      non-degenerate on $(k_\tau, k_x, k_y)$, and that its propagation has a finite characteristic cone with~$\tau$ as
      the single ordering direction.
      Under~[H-L], [H-lift] and~[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$], in homogeneous and quasi-isotropic admissible regimes, the
      non-degenerate block $(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H)$ has signature $(-,+,+)$ with~$\tau$ time-like.
      In three variables, the argument excludes the definite class by the finite-cone clause, a Lorentzian class with
      a spatial time-like direction by the ordering clause, and the degenerate or parabolic class by the
      non-degeneracy clause.
      The signs of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ are stipulated, not derived: the theorem records that they are the only
      ones compatible with a finite characteristic cone ordered by~$\tau$ (Q5b Remark~6.2).
      The hyperbolicity hypothesis is an explicit assumption; the capacity bound alone does not force hyperbolicity,
      flux-limited diffusion being a counterexample~\cite{Beau2026c}.
      A diagonal extension $\mathrm{diag}(-A_\tau, A_H, A_H, A_z)$ with $A_z > 0$ in the left-invariant frame would have
      the signature $(-,+,+,+)$; its supply is the open problem Q5b-O2.
      The present paper carries this conditional statement forward and does not re-establish it.

  \section{Effective Spacetime Geometry}
  \label{sec:geometry}

  \subsection{The effective co-metric}
    \label{sec:metric}

    Under the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:status}, Q5b Theorem~5.2 gives the rank-three
    co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol}, and Q5b Theorem~6.1 gives the signature $(-,+,+)$ of its non-degenerate
    block under~[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$].
    A four-dimensional Lorentzian metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ is available only for a supplied rank-four extension that is
    Lorentzian, for instance diagonal with $A_z > 0$ under~[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] (Q5b-O2);
    the statements below that use one are conditional on that supply.
    The co-metric is defined on the effective continuum $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ and
    encodes how admissible modes would propagate in the continuum limit posited by~[H-L].

    \begin{remark}[Nature of the effective four-dimensionality]
      \label{rem:4d}
      \setlength{\emergencystretch}{1em}
      The effective four-dimensionality is not that of a non-degenerate pseudo-Riemannian manifold at the
      principal-symbol level.
      It arises from the homogeneous dimension $D_{\mathrm{hom}} = 4$ of the underlying Carnot structure (Q5b
      Theorem~3.2), which governs both volume growth and spectral scaling.
      The co-metric extracted from the principal symbol is an effective object, valid after projection onto
      admissible continuum configurations; it is not a smooth metric at every point of the sub-Riemannian space
      $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$ without further hypotheses.
      Extending the construction to a full $4\times 4$ non-degenerate Lorentzian metric that incorporates the
      central direction~$Z$ is the content of Q5b-O2.
    \end{remark}

    \paragraph{Relation to Hamilton--Jacobi dynamics.}
      Once the effective co-metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ has been read off from the principal symbol of
      $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (equations~\eqref{eq:principal-symbol}--\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol}), or a
      rank-four extension of it has been supplied,
      the associated eikonal equation
      \[
        g^{\mu\nu}(x)\,\partial_\mu S\,\partial_\nu S \;=\; 0
      \]
      is the characteristic (eikonal) equation of $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$; for a supplied rank-four extension it
      is the Hamilton--Jacobi equation for massless propagation in the effective geometry~\cite{Arnold1989}.
      This identification belongs to the already projected geometric regime: it does not define admissibility
      itself, but describes the characteristic propagation structure once the effective co-metric has been
      reconstructed.
      Hamilton--Jacobi theory should therefore be understood here as an effective reformulation of the
      projected dynamics, not as a primitive layer of the framework.
      The primitive admissibility relation constructs the effective configuration space on which the
      Hamilton--Jacobi description subsequently becomes meaningful.

  \subsection{Newtonian exterior, conditional Schwarzschild form}
  \label{sec:schwarzschild}

  The signature statement of Section~\ref{sec:metric} holds in homogeneous and quasi-isotropic admissible
  regimes.
  A localised stationary obstruction to the admissibility flow breaks homogeneity.
  For a supplied rank-four extension that is spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat, which Q5b does not
  provide, flux conservation gives a Newtonian radial profile, and a stationary exterior metric needs further
  dynamical input.

  \begin{proposition}[Newtonian exterior and conditional Schwarzschild form, after~\cite{Beau2026c}]
    \label{thm:schwarzschild}
    Assume a supplied rank-four extension of the co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol} which, in the exterior of a
    localised stationary obstruction, is spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat, so that the exterior operator
    takes the radial form $\nabla_\mu\bigl(A^{\mu\nu}(r)\nabla_\nu\bigr)$ with
    $A^{\mu\nu}(r) = \mathrm{diag}\bigl(-A_t(r), A_r(r), A_\perp(r), A_\perp(r)\bigr)$.
    Q5b provides no such extension, and no left-invariant extension of~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol} is one.
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item[(i)] \emph{Newtonian exterior.}
      If the exterior is asymptotically homogeneous, $A_r(r) \to 1$, a stationary probe $\Phi$ obeying flux
      conservation has the leading exterior profile $\Phi(r) = \Phi_0 - C/r$, with $C$ an integration constant
      measuring the strength of the obstruction.
      \item[(ii)] \emph{Schwarzschild form, under additional input.}
      If moreover $A_r \equiv 1$ in the exterior, and the static line element
      $-f_t\,dt^2 + f_r\,dr^2 + r^2\,d\Omega^2$ is subject to the closure $f_t f_r = 1$ and to the exact temporal
      identification $f_t = 1 + 2\Phi$ with $\Phi_0 = 0$, both supplied as external dynamical input, then the exterior
      is the Schwarzschild metric
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:schwarzschild}
        ds^2 \;=\; -\!\left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)dt^2
        + \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1}\!dr^2
        + r^2\,d\Omega^2,
        \qquad r_s = 2C
      \end{equation}
      in relational units.
      With the weak-field identification alone, this form holds only to leading order in $C/r$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Neither the extension nor the input of~(ii) is derived in the present paper.
  \end{proposition}

  \begin{proof}
    Under the stated hypotheses, the exterior equation $\nabla_\mu(A^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu\Phi) = 0$ for a stationary
    probe reduces to radial flux conservation through spheres of radius~$r$,
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:flux-conservation}
      \frac{1}{r^2}\,\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2 A_r(r)\,\frac{d\Phi}{dr}\right) \;=\; 0,
      \qquad\text{that is,}\qquad r^2 A_r(r)\,\frac{d\Phi}{dr} = C.
    \end{equation}
    With $A_r \to 1$ this gives~(i) up to corrections of order $\sup_{r' \ge r}\lvert A_r(r') - 1\rvert\, C/r$, and
    with $A_r \equiv 1$ it gives $\Phi = \Phi_0 - C/r$ exactly.
    For~(ii), the temporal identification with $\Phi_0 = 0$ gives $f_t = 1 - 2C/r$, and the closure gives
    $f_r = f_t^{-1}$, which is~\eqref{eq:schwarzschild}.
    The closure is not a coordinate choice: given the asymptotic normalisation it is a dynamical condition, equivalent
    to $R^t{}_t = R^r{}_r$, and the metric $f_t = 1 - r_s/r$, $f_r = 1$ satisfies every constraint used in~(i).
    The argument is that of Section~8 of~\cite{Beau2026c}; the present statement transcribes it to a supplied
    extension of the Q5b co-metric.
  \end{proof}

  \begin{remark}[Other stationary exteriors; no uniqueness claim]
    \label{rem:schwarzschild-uniqueness}
    Flux conservation does not single out the Schwarzschild form: the closure of
    Proposition~\ref{thm:schwarzschild}(ii) is input.
    Stationary spherically symmetric exteriors such as the Schwarzschild\textendash de Sitter and
    Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om families also satisfy the closure, and carry a further parameter (a cosmological
    constant, a charge) associated with an additional scale.
    Within Proposition~\ref{thm:schwarzschild}(ii) they are excluded only by the supplied inputs, exterior homogeneity
    and the exact temporal identification $f_t = 1 + 2\Phi$; no premise derived here excludes them.
    In particular, a single-scale rigidity of the admissibility structure is not established, and the geometric
    sector already carries independent scales: a full-rank extension of the co-metric carries a length scale
    (Q5b Remark~5.5), the temporal step is a free datum (Q11~\cite{Beau2026q11}), and the Einstein and cosmological
    coefficients are matching data~\cite{Beau2026h,Beau2026Thermodynamics}.
    No uniqueness statement is therefore made.
  \end{remark}

  \subsection{The horizon: an interpretive reading}
  \label{sec:horizon}

  \begin{remark}[Horizon as a chart-relative degeneracy; interpretation]
    \label{rem:horizon}
    In the conditional Schwarzschild exterior, the static chart adapted to the ordering degenerates at $r = r_s$: the
    temporal coefficient $f_t$ vanishes while curvature invariants remain finite.
    This degeneracy is chart-relative: charts regular across $r_s$ exist, and no invariant breakdown of the effective
    operator occurs at the horizon~\cite{Beau2026c}.
    If the ordering $\tau$ defines a physically preferred foliation, the horizon can be read as the locus where that
    slicing ceases to be transverse to effective propagation.
    Reading it as capacity saturation would additionally require a derived relation between the flux scale and the
    capacity bound $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, which is not available.
    These readings are interpretation, not results.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Relation to the Gravity paper]
    \label{rem:gravity-horizon}
    The spectral functional of the Gravity paper~\cite{Beau2026h} is constructed for a Laplace-type operator on
    Riemannian backgrounds, by heat-kernel and zeta-regularisation methods.
    A Lorentzian counterpart, and with it any treatment of horizon geometries within that functional, is open: it would
    require an in-in construction on a closed time path~\cite{Beau2026i}.
    The interpretive reading of Remark~\ref{rem:horizon} does not depend on it.
  \end{remark}

  \section{Relation to the Einstein Equations}
  \label{sec:einstein}

  The exterior of Proposition~\ref{thm:schwarzschild} is a specific stationary solution, obtained with external
  dynamical input.
  A relation to the Einstein equations in general is the subject of the Gravity paper~\cite{Beau2026h} and of the
  spectral-stationarity paper~\cite{Beau2026Thermodynamics}, under their own conditions.

  \begin{remark}[Conditional Einstein response from the spectral functional]
    \label{rem:einstein}
    The Gravity paper~\cite{Beau2026h} takes a Laplace-type operator $A_g = -\nabla_g^2$ on a four-dimensional
    Riemannian manifold as its starting point and studies the functional
    \begin{equation}
      S_\Pi[g] \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det{}' A_g .
    \end{equation}
    In a proper-time cutoff scheme, one minimal scalar contributes $\Delta c_{\mathrm{EH}} = -\Lambda^2/[12(4\pi)^2]$
    to the Einstein\textendash Hilbert coefficient; the finite renormalised coefficient, its sign and Newton's constant
    require a matching condition and are not predicted.
    Once these coefficients are supplied, the Einstein tensor is the dominant local two-derivative metric response
    when the renormalised higher-derivative ratios are small.
    Identifying $A_g$ with the effective operator of the present paper would require the supplied rank-four extension
    and a Lorentzian formulation, neither of which is available.
    The companion paper~\cite{Beau2026Thermodynamics}, also working on a Riemannian four-manifold, shows that, assuming
    a renormalised infrared metric response and
    an effective matter functional $W_\Pi$, stationarity of $\Gamma_\Pi = S_\Pi^{\mathrm{ren}} - W_\Pi$ is equivalent,
    at leading infrared order, to $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}} g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G_N T^{(\Pi)}_{\mu\nu}$.
    It also shows that no local first law follows from the heat kernel alone: the diagonal heat kernel determines
    neither a physical temperature nor a heat density without an additional calibration map.
    Reading these equations as consistency conditions of the emergent geometry, with $T^{(\Pi)}_{\mu\nu}$ encoding
    localised obstructions to the admissibility flow, is an interpretation, not a result.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Status of the chain]
    \label{rem:chain}
    The chain
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:chain}
      \Pi_q
      \;\xrightarrow{\;[\mathrm{H\text{-}L}]\;}\;
      \mathcal{L}_\Pi
      \;\xrightarrow{\;[\mathrm{H\text{-}lift}]\;}\;
      g^{\mu\nu}_{(3)}
      \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{Q5b-O2}\;}\;
      g^{\mu\nu}
      \;\xrightarrow{\;\text{\cite{Beau2026h}}\;}\;
      G_{\mu\nu}
    \end{equation}
    is conditional at each arrow.
    The first arrow is the unestablished hypothesis~[H-L].
    The second is Q5b Theorem~5.2 under the open hypothesis~[H-lift], with the temporal term of
    $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ stipulated on the modelling product
    $\mathbb{R}_\tau \times \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R})$; its output $g^{\mu\nu}_{(3)}$ is the rank-three
    co-metric~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol}.
    The third is a supplied rank-four extension, whose construction is the open problem Q5b-O2; it is Lorentzian,
    with~$\tau$ the only time-like direction, if it is diagonal in the left-invariant frame with $A_z > 0$ and
    [H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$] holds (Q5b Theorem~6.1), whereas an extension with horizontal\textendash central cross
    terms need not be.
    The fourth is the variational argument of~\cite{Beau2026h}, which is performed on Riemannian backgrounds and whose
    Einstein coefficient is a matching datum; applying it to the Lorentzian metric of the third arrow requires a
    Lorentzian formulation that is not supplied, and the field equations need in addition the effective matter
    functional of~\cite{Beau2026Thermodynamics}, also stated on Riemannian backgrounds.
    The present paper does not supply the third arrow: identifying the rank-three object of Q5b with the metric of the
    variational argument of~\cite{Beau2026h} requires that extension.
    No value of the co-metric coefficients is established (Section~\ref{par:cometric-metric}).
  \end{remark}

  \section{Open Directions}
  \label{sec:open}

  \paragraph{Remaining open directions.}
    \textbf{Establishing or replacing [H-L].}
    No source of the spatial limit operator~\eqref{eq:Lpi-shadow} is identified in the corpus.
    Establishing it, or replacing it by another spatial input, is the open content of question Q5 of the Foundation
    programme, and every geometric statement of this paper depends on it.

    \textbf{Q5b-O1 ([H-lift]).}
    Open; Q9~\cite{Beau2026q9} gives sufficient conditions for a kinetic Mosco limit and does not discharge it.

    \textbf{Q5b-O2 (full-rank extension).}
    Open.
    The Heisenberg commutator supplies no central coefficient (Q8~\cite{BeauQ8}, Proposition~3.1), and a full-rank
    extension requires a new operator containing a central term of homogeneous degree four, together with the length
    scale that such a term carries (Q5b Remark~5.5).
    No value of a central coefficient $A_z$ is established.

    \textbf{Q5b-O3 (coefficient values).}
    Open.
    No value of $A_\tau$ or $A_H$ is established: Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} records that Q10 derives no value of $A_H$,
    that, for a supplied positive rank-three form invariant under the full $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ action, Q8 leaves the
    common value of the coefficients free, and that Q11 shows spatial
    $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ invariance to leave $A_\tau$ independent of the spatial coefficients.

  \paragraph{Additional scales in the exterior.}
    Whether further dimensional data enter the exterior problem, and so admit Schwarzschild\textendash de Sitter or
    Reissner\textendash Nordstr\"om type exteriors, is open.
    Since the geometric sector already carries independent scales (Remark~\ref{rem:schwarzschild-uniqueness}), a
    single-scale rigidity of the admissibility structure is not available as a premise.

  \paragraph{Cosmological regime.}
    In homogeneous regimes, the white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} relates a linear growth of cumulative projected capacity
    $I(n)$ to the emergent Hubble law and effective cosmological expansion; that relation is not examined here.
    The precise connection between the spectral exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ of the O-series~\cite{Beau2026a28}
    and the observable cosmological parameters ($H_0$, $w$) is an open quantitative problem.

  \paragraph{Relation to the spectral entropy programme.}
    The present paper assembles, under~[H-L] and~[H-lift], the rank-three co-metric read off from the principal
    symbol of the effective operator, whose non-degenerate block has signature $(-,+,+)$
    under~[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$].
    Any relation of this geometry, through a supplied rank-four extension, to the Einstein tensor and the
    Yang\textendash Mills sector of the renormalised spectral entropy functional
    \[
      S_\Pi[g,A] \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det{}' A_{g,A}
    \]
    belongs to the companion spectral-variation programme of the Gravity
    paper~\cite{Beau2026h}, Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, and Q13~\cite{Beau2026q13}, where the geometric and gauge
    responses appear at successive Seeley\textendash DeWitt orders.

  \section{Conclusion}
  \label{sec:conclusion}

    Under~[H-L], [H-lift] and~[H-hyp$_{\mathrm{red}}$], the posited continuum operator has a rank-three principal
    symbol whose non-degenerate block is Lorentzian; a four-dimensional Lorentzian metric requires a supplied
    extension (Q5b-O2).
    The Newtonian exterior profile and the Schwarzschild form of the Born\textendash Infeld paper require, on the
    Heisenberg carrier, a supplied spherically symmetric and asymptotically flat extension, which Q5b does not
    provide; the Schwarzschild form also needs external dynamical input, the Einstein response needs matching data,
    and the field equations need an effective matter functional.
    The variational construction does not by itself provide a local first law: the diagonal heat
    kernel determines neither a physical temperature nor a heat density without an additional
    calibration map.

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
    The conditional results are compatible with treating spacetime geometry as an effective propagation structure
    selected by admissibility; they do not show that admissibility selects it.
    It does not establish that geometry, thermodynamic temperature, and matter calibration emerge
    from one local scalar datum; those bridges remain separate questions.
