  \begin{abstract}
    Continuing from the gauge structure established in the companion
    paper~\cite{Q6a}, we derive effective spacetime geometry from the admissibility
    framework of~\cite{FoundationM} without introducing it as a postulate.
    In the continuum limit $q \to \infty$ of~\cite{Q5a}, the discrete admissibility
    filter $\Pi_n$ converges to an effective continuum operator $\mathcal{L}_\Pi$
    whose principal symbol defines an effective metric tensor
    $g^{\mu\nu}(x) \propto A^{\mu\nu}(x)$.
    The bounded Born\textendash Infeld admissibility constraint (A3--A4) restricts
    the admissible forms of $A^{\mu\nu}(x)$: in homogeneous regimes, hyperbolicity
    and spectral stability uniquely select Minkowski geometry; in the presence of a
    localised stationary obstruction, flux conservation forces the Schwarzschild
    geometry as the unique stationary exterior solution.
    The Einstein equations emerge as consistency conditions of emergent geometry,
    with the Einstein\textendash Hilbert action derived rigorously
    in~\cite{Gravity}.
    No dynamical substrate is required at any step.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:intro}

    \subsection*{Position in the programme}

      The companion paper~\cite{Q6a} derives gauge charges, the gauge group, and
      Yang\textendash Mills dynamics from the admissibility structure of non-injective
      projection.
      The present paper addresses the complementary macroscopic question: what
      effective geometry is selected when the admissibility constraints
      of~\cite{FoundationM} are imposed on a spatially varying projection?
      The answer is Lorentzian geometry with Schwarzschild as the exterior stationary
      solution (§\ref{sec:geometry}).

    \subsection*{What is and is not derived in this paper}

      This paper derives the \emph{structure} of effective spacetime geometry from the
      admissibility framework.
      It does not derive the precise numerical value of Newton's constant or
      quantitative cosmological parameters.
      The rigorous Mosco convergence establishing the continuum limit is established
      conditionally in~\cite{Q5a}; the present paper uses it as a working hypothesis
      throughout.

    \subsection*{Organisation}

      Section~\ref{sec:continuum} establishes the continuum limit of the discrete
      projection $\Pi_n$ and the emergence of an effective differential operator.
      Section~\ref{sec:geometry} derives the effective metric from the principal
      symbol of $\mathcal{L}_\Pi$ and establishes the emergence of Minkowski and
      Schwarzschild geometries.
      Section~\ref{sec:open} identifies the open directions.

  \section{Continuum Limit of the Admissibility Projection}
    \label{sec:continuum}

    \subsection{From discrete $\Pi_n$ to a continuum operator}

      The discrete projection $\Pi_n$ acts on the orbit span
      $\Omega_n^{(c)} \subset \mathbb{C}^q$, filtering admissible directions via the
      Born\textendash Infeld bound $A_n \leq c_\chi/\sqrt{\lambda_n}$
      (Foundation~M, Axiom~A3).
      As established in~\cite{FoundationM} §3.5, the large-$q$ limit sends:
      \begin{align}
        G_q &\;\longrightarrow\; \mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{R}),\\
        \mathbb{C}^q &\;\longrightarrow\; L^2(\mathbb{R}),\\
        \text{BFS shells } S_n &\;\longrightarrow\; \text{continuous foliation},\\
        \Pi_n \text{ (discrete)} &\;\longrightarrow\; \mathcal{L}_\Pi
        \text{ (continuum operator)}.
      \end{align}

      The continuum limit of relational Laplacians on graphs of the type
      $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ is governed by standard spectral convergence
      results~\cite{BornInfeld}: under mild regularity conditions, the graph Laplacian
      converges in the strong-resolvent sense to a second-order differential operator
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:continuum-op}
        \mathcal{L}_\Pi \;=\; \nabla_\mu\bigl(A^{\mu\nu}(x)\,\nabla_\nu\bigr),
      \end{equation}
      where $A^{\mu\nu}(x)$ is a symmetric tensor encoding the local connectivity
      and stiffness of the admissible projection.

      \begin{remark}[Status of the continuum limit]
        The convergence $\Pi_n \to \mathcal{L}_\Pi$ is established qualitatively
        in~\cite{FoundationM} and rigorously for the Heisenberg Cayley graph
        $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ conditionally in~\cite{Q5a} (Theorem~7.3, under
        Hypotheses H1, H-w, H-E1, and numerically confirmed Conjecture~C).
        The present section uses this result as a working hypothesis.
        A fully rigorous proof closing the remaining hypotheses H1 and H2 is
        identified as an open direction in Section~\ref{sec:open}.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{The operator-metric correspondence}

      In admissible continuum regimes, the operator $\mathcal{L}_\Pi$ has a
      well-defined principal symbol:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:principal-symbol}
        \sigma_2(\mathcal{L}_\Pi)(x, k)
        \;=\; A^{\mu\nu}(x)\,k_\mu k_\nu.
      \end{equation}
      This symbol defines an effective metric tensor~\cite{BornInfeld}:
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:metric-from-symbol}
        g^{\mu\nu}(x) \;\propto\; A^{\mu\nu}(x).
      \end{equation}
      The metric is not an independent dynamical variable: it is a derived object
      encoding how admissible modes propagate in the continuum limit.
      This is the operator-metric correspondence on which all subsequent geometric
      results rest.

  \section{Effective Spacetime Geometry}
    \label{sec:geometry}

    \subsection{Metric from the principal symbol}

      The operator-metric correspondence~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol} gives the
      effective metric as $g^{\mu\nu}(x) \propto A^{\mu\nu}(x)$.
      This metric is not postulated: it encodes how admissible modes propagate in
      the continuum limit of the projection.

    \subsection{Signature selection from admissibility}

      \begin{theorem}[Lorentzian signature from spectral admissibility,
        after~\cite{BornInfeld}]
        \label{thm:signature}
        In homogeneous and quasi-isotropic admissible regimes, the
        Born\textendash Infeld admissibility constraint (A3--A4 of~\cite{FoundationM})
        forces the effective metric to have Lorentzian signature $(-,+,+,+)$.
      \end{theorem}

      \begin{proof}
        The bounded Born\textendash Infeld flux constraint excludes purely diffusive
        (parabolic) effective descriptions, which would imply instantaneous propagation
        of perturbations and contradict the finite capacity bound of A4.
        A Riemannian signature $(+,+,+,+)$ produces an elliptic principal symbol,
        which is also excluded.
        An ultra-hyperbolic signature (more than one time direction) fails to admit a
        globally well-posed initial value problem and is excluded by the spectral
        stability requirement.
        The unique remaining option compatible with (i) hyperbolicity of
        $\sigma_2(\mathcal{L}_\Pi)$, (ii) finite characteristic cone,
        (iii) the monotonic temporal ordering of Foundation~M §4.3, and (iv) the
        bounded admissibility constraint of A4, is the Lorentzian signature
        $(-,+,+,+)$.
        The detailed argument is established in~\cite{BornInfeld} Sections~5--6,
        which depend only on the bounded-flux constraint and spectral stability ---
        both present in the Foundation~M axioms --- and not on any dynamical
        substrate.
      \end{proof}

    \subsection{Schwarzschild geometry from flux conservation}

      \begin{theorem}[Schwarzschild from bounded admissibility,
        after~\cite{BornInfeld}]
        \label{thm:schwarzschild}
        In the presence of a localised stationary obstruction to the admissibility
        flow, with spherical symmetry and no additional microscopic scales, the unique
        exterior stationary solution of the effective geometry induced by
        $\mathcal{L}_\Pi$ is the Schwarzschild metric
        \begin{equation}
          \label{eq:schwarzschild}
          ds^2
          \;=\; -\!\left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)dt^2
          + \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2
          + r^2 d\Omega^2,
        \end{equation}
        where $r_s$ is an integration constant characterising the strength of the
        obstruction.
      \end{theorem}

      \begin{proof}
        In the exterior region, the homogeneous equation for the effective
        operator~\eqref{eq:continuum-op} under spherical symmetry reduces to
        \begin{equation}
          \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}\!\left(r^2 A^r(r)\frac{d\Phi}{dr}\right) = 0,
        \end{equation}
        expressing conservation of admissible flux through spheres of radius $r$.
        The general solution is $\Phi(r) = \Phi_0 - C/r$, giving a $1/r$ decay
        profile.
        Via the operator-metric correspondence~\eqref{eq:metric-from-symbol}, this
        profile translates to the metric coefficients $g_{tt} = -f(r)$,
        $g_{rr} = f(r)^{-1}$ with $f(r) = 1 - r_s/r$.
        The parameter $r_s$ appears as the integration constant of the flux
        conservation equation and is identified operationally via its influence on
        admissible propagation cones.
        This derivation requires only: (i) the bounded-flux constraint (A3--A4),
        (ii) local injectivity of the projection in the exterior region,
        (iii) spherical symmetry and stationarity, (iv) absence of additional
        microscopic scales.
        No independent metric field equations or energy-momentum tensor are postulated.
        The detailed argument is established in~\cite{BornInfeld} Sections~6--8.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Horizon as projection degeneracy]
        At $r = r_s$, the lapse function $f(r_s) = 0$.
        In the present framework, this corresponds to a degeneracy of the principal
        symbol $\sigma_2(\mathcal{L}_\Pi)$: the operator loses hyperbolicity and the
        continuum chart ceases to be projectable.
        The horizon is therefore not a physical singularity of the underlying
        admissible structure --- it is the breakdown of the effective continuum
        description.
        This interpretation is consistent with the non-injectivity result
        of~\cite{ENI}: at the horizon, distinct microscopic configurations become
        maximally indistinguishable, maximising the projection entropy $S_\Pi$.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{Einstein equations as consistency conditions}

      \begin{remark}[Einstein equations from the spectral entropy functional]
        \label{rem:einstein}
        The paper~\cite{Gravity} shows rigorously, via heat-kernel methods and zeta
        regularisation, that the renormalized metric variation of the projective entropy
        functional $S_\Pi[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' \mathcal{L}_\Pi$ produces, in the
        infrared-dominant two-derivative sector, a response proportional to the Einstein
        tensor $G_{\mu\nu}$ via the heat-kernel mechanism of Sakharov-induced gravity.
        The Einstein equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi T_{\mu\nu}$ therefore emerge as
        consistency conditions of emergent geometry --- not as microscopic laws of the
        admissible substrate.
        In the language of the present paper: they express the balance between the
        curvature of the projection-induced metric (encoding admissibility constraints)
        and the energy-momentum content (encoding localised obstructions to the
        admissibility flow).
        The white paper~\cite{Cosmochrony} provides the broader programme context.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Open Directions}
    \label{sec:open}

    \paragraph{Rigorous continuum limit.}
      The convergence $\Pi_n \to \mathcal{L}_\Pi$ for the Heisenberg Cayley graph
      $\mathrm{Cay}(G_q, S_q)$ in the $q \to \infty$ limit is established
      conditionally in~\cite{Q5a}.
      Closing the remaining hypotheses H1 and H2 and Conjecture~C would consolidate
      the geometric results of the present paper.

    \paragraph{Identification of $A$ from admissibility data.}
      The constant $A > 0$ in the continuum Dirichlet form
      $\mathcal{E}(f,g) = A\int \partial_\mu f\,\partial^\mu g\,d^4x$ must be
      expressed in terms of the Born\textendash Infeld saturation constant $c_\chi$
      and the BFS combinatorial data of $G_q$ (Q5a open problem~O5).
      This identification closes the chain from the spectral data to the effective
      Newton constant $G_N \sim 16\pi^2 \ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2$ of~\cite{Gravity}.

    \paragraph{Coupling constants.}
      The present paper identifies the admissible geometry.
      It does not determine the numerical values of $G_N$ or observable cosmological
      parameters.
      These are expected to emerge from the quantitative spectral data of the O-series
      via the transfer chain $c_\chi \to \delta_{\mathrm{pair}} \to \beta^* \approx 0.126$,
      but the explicit identification is open.

    \paragraph{Cosmological implications.}
      In homogeneous regimes, the admissibility flow gives a linear growth of
      cumulative projected capacity $I(n)$, underlying the emergent Hubble law and
      effective cosmological expansion~\cite{Cosmochrony}.
      The precise connection between the spectral exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$
      and the observable cosmological parameters ($H_0$, $w$) is an open quantitative
      problem.
